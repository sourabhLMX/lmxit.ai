The problem

Every distributor runs a delivery operation. None of them wants to.

Your own vehicles, your own drivers, your own dispatcher on their own phone. It's a cost you absorb to protect a sale — not a business you chose to be in. And it's the most expensive department you never budgeted for.

01
Fixed cost, variable demand.

You pay for eight hours of driver and van whether Tuesday brings 20 drops or 60.

02
Nobody knows the real number.

Wages, overtime, insurance, fuel, repairs, dispatch time — spread over five budget lines, never added up per delivery.

03
Nobody commits to a time.

Ask a distributor for a delivery guarantee and you won't get one. They can't make it, so they don't offer it — and the shop calls your counter instead.

How it works

Zero technical work on your end. Four steps, and you're not running delivery anymore.

STEP 01

You keep doing business as usual

Phone, email, a spreadsheet, or a system like Epicor — whatever you already use to take orders today. Nothing changes for your counter.

STEP 02

We build the connection

Our team sets up the link on our side. No software to install, no IT team required from you. Every order you take shows up with us automatically — no double entry, ever.

STEP 03

We group, route and deliver

Our dispatch system holds each order just long enough to combine nearby drops, then re-plans the whole fleet as new orders land. Hot parts still leave now; everything else rides together.

STEP 04

You watch it happen

A clean portal shows every order against its committed window, with photo proof. One monthly invoice: completed drops × your rate. That's the entire bill.

Bottom line: we handle 100% of the setup and 100% of the driving. You keep running your business exactly the way you do today — minus the fleet.

Questions

Straight answers.

What happens to my current drivers and vans?
That's your call, and we plan the transition with you. Many customers redeploy drivers to the counter or warehouse, return leased vehicles at renewal, and switch delivery to LMX zone by zone rather than overnight. We can run alongside your team while you wind down, and nothing moves until you've compared the numbers.
How do you set my per-drop rate?
Three inputs: your delivery area (how far drops typically go), your daily volume, and what you ship (size, weight, handling). Denser, more predictable volume earns a lower rate; standing routes and committed programmes lower it further. The rate is fixed in your contract, so for the first time you can budget delivery as a cost per order.
What does "committed delivery window" actually mean?
Each order is assigned a window based on its urgency — a hot-shot part to a shop with a car on the lift is not the same as stock replenishment. We track every drop against that window and report it back to you. Our contract sets out what happens when we miss one, so the commitment has teeth.
Do I need new software? Will my team have to learn something?
No. We connect to the order or point-of-sale system you already use — Epicor today, others as we go — or accept a simple order feed. Your staff keep working the way they do now. The only new thing is a portal showing every delivery, and fewer phone calls.
Can you handle returns and core exchanges?
Yes. Return legs — including warranty cores in the auto-parts world — are planned into the route, so the part that needs to come back does, on the same visit.
